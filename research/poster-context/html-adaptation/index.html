Poster + source

An AI assistant can write analysis code. Can another person check its result, rerun the work, and reuse what it produced? STAMPED gives us seven practical questions to ask about that research.

We compare the same tasks with and without five reusable skills.A skill is a short set of instructions and checks an assistant can follow. We check scientific results and the records needed to reproduce them.

2 · A small, increasingly demanding case study

How do response times change across runs?

Study + evidence

We use public mixed-gambles task records from OpenNeuro ds000005: 16 participants and three runs each. We summarize recorded decision times; we do not infer why they changed.

Checked scientific results

One participant: median response time 1272 → 1261 ms; change -11 ms. Both arms agree.

16 participants: average within-person change +31.3 ms; 95% bootstrap interval [-70.3, 132.7] ms. Both arms agree.

Change is third minus first run; the interval includes zero. Missing responses limit interpretation. This is not a causal effect.

Sensitivity: median/mean summaries, with or without a 0.2–3.0 s response-time band, gave +11.2 to +58.7 ms; all four 95% intervals include zero.

Scope change: public behavioral data replace the proposed ABCD neuroimaging case. The ABCD result has not been reproduced.

Screening and integration failures

The skills arm in task 03 failed the required case-insensitive “N/A” check. Those tokens were absent from the real data. Missing validation software and native DataLad rerun also caused failures.

Limits of this study

All trials share an account, host, and software caches. Fresh contexts do not isolate account settings.

An unblinded study-design agent checked the outputs; the authors develop STAMPED and these skills. This small case series cannot establish general effectiveness.
